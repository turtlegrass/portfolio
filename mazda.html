The Used Import Pipeline: Why Toyota & Ford Dominate

How thirty years of salvaged vehicle imports created competitor market share, and how Mazda systematically dismantles it[cite: 1].

Three Decades of Fleet Conditioning

1. The Thirty-Year Used Fleet Inflow

Between 1993 and 2018, over 80% of vehicle imports were used US auction inventory, dominated by Camrys, Corollas, and Priuses. This formed a vast national fleet where roadside mechanics in every province learned to service Toyota systems using widely available scrap parts[cite: 1].

2. The Resulting Resale Liquidity Belief

Because roadside repair was universally available, buyers treated used Toyotas as cash-equivalent assets that could be resold within 48 hours[cite: 1]. Toyota's dominance in new car showrooms is the direct result of this used fleet familiarity rather than modern vehicle design[cite: 1].

3. The Mazda Turnaround Strategy

Mazda cannot replicate thirty years of grey-market imports overnight. Instead, we remove customer hesitation with written 36-month Guaranteed Buy-Back Certificates in Khmer[cite: 1]. We support this with 25 certified independent repair shops via the Automotive Centre of Excellence Cambodia (ACE-C), alongside trade-in incentives for older Toyotas[cite: 1].

Partnering with ACE-C allows Mazda to train and certify 25 independent provincial workshops during year one[cite: 1]. We supply diagnostic units, maintain genuine parts lines, and install official exterior signage[cite: 1]. This matches competitor service accessibility without the capital expense of full franchise showrooms[cite: 1].
