01 Review · Marketing Audit

Marketing Review: Three Main Problems

We reviewed 1,672 past social media posts and promotional materials. The results show three clear operational problems: inconsistent branding, zero mention of our vehicle safety, and low customer engagement caused by irregular posting schedules.

Identified Operational Issues

Issue 01 Severity: High

Inconsistent Brand Identity

Marketing designs did not follow Mazda's official global guidelines. Fonts changed constantly, car photos used inaccurate colors, and dealership posters mixed conflicting visual styles.

Action: Set up standardized Khmer fonts and uniform showroom templates.
Issue 02 Severity: Critical

No Focus on Safety

Mazda won eight IIHS Top Safety Pick+ awards, outperforming the entire industry. However, past local marketing never mentioned crash-test ratings, reinforced steel frames, or driver-assist systems.

Action: Translate crash-test videos into Khmer and run public vehicle safety checks.
Issue 03 Severity: Critical

Weak Content & Inactive Periods

Posts averaged only 0.29 likes and shares. Nearly half of the posts had no descriptions or prices, and the page stopped posting completely for 94 days in 2025.

Action: Post three times a week with clear Khmer descriptions and transparent monthly financing prices ($492/month).

03 Market Analysis · Consumer Habits

The Used Car Market: Why Competitors Lead

Competitors hold a large market share because of 30 years of used car imports. These imports trained local mechanics and created a strong belief that used Japanese cars are easy to resell anywhere in the country.

How Market Habits Developed (1993 - Present)

Phase 01 1993 - 2008

Massive Inflow of Used Vehicles

Between 1993 and 2018, over 80% of vehicle imports were used cars from American auto auctions that arrived via the port of Sihanoukville. This built a huge customer base without any official dealership advertising.

Phase 02 2005 - 2018

Familiar Mechanics & Spare Parts

An entire generation of provincial mechanics learned to repair only these specific engines with basic tools. Spare part markets in Phnom Penh, such as Toul Sangke, provided fast, cheap replacement parts.

Phase 03 2015 - 2024

High Resale Value & Buyer Habits

Because cars could be repaired easily anywhere, people treated them like cash savings that could be resold at any time. When buyers finally upgraded to brand-new cars (which now make up 66% of sales), they stayed with the brands they already knew.

Phase 04 Our Solution

The Turnaround Plan

Mazda will remove resale fears with formal, 36-month Buy-Back Agreements written in Khmer. We are also certifying 25 independent repair shops through the Automotive Centre of Excellence Cambodia (ACE-C), and offering an entry-level CX-30 starting at $29,999 ($492/month).

By partnering with ACE-C, Mazda can train and certify 25 independent repair shops in the provinces during Year 1. We will supply diagnostic tools, genuine spare parts, and official Mazda signage. This matches competitor service coverage across the country without the high cost of opening full brand showrooms.
